# The cat's house

Brain's cat has a small house. It is a Labs feature: turn on **Settings → Labs →
Cat** and a tab of its own, **Cat**, appears beside Brain and Sessions at the top
of the App; turn it off and the tab goes away. Tap the tab and you are there. It
needs no other setup and has no menus: you tap things in the picture. Swipe to
Brain or Sessions, or tap their tabs, to leave.

The switch belongs to the computer, not the phone: it is kept with the cat's own
state in the daemon, so the App and the web UI on that computer always agree,
and switching computers shows that computer's choice. It starts off. While it is
off the cat stays home and does not follow Brain's Work; turned on, it joins the
Work running then, and Work that came and went while it was off sends no
postcards.

The house is one lived-in room painted in gouache: a round door with a doormat,
a peg with its satchel, a desk, a tea table, a stove, a dining table, a bed and
a rug, with a day and a night picture that fade into each other with the clock.
The cat is one round, striped silhouette; it only changes pose. The room is
taller and wider than a phone, and the view drifts sideways to keep the cat in
sight.

## The loop

Brain works, the cat travels, it writes to you, you look after it at home, and
the bond that grows changes what it does and what it brings back.

| Brain | The cat |
| --- | --- |
| Idle | At home, living its day by the phone's clock (see below) |
| A Worker starts a Work | It takes its satchel off the peg and leaves by the door, taking the snack you packed |
| The Work runs | A postcard shortly after it leaves; it comes home on its own after a short outing, and may go out once more if the Work runs long |
| The Work is accepted | A souvenir, which it puts somewhere in the room, and often a new hobby; if the cat is out on that Work, it comes home with it |
| Something waits on you | A small sign hangs on the door. Tap it to go back to Brain |

A Work that is cancelled brings nothing home.

### Outings

The cat follows every Work, but it is out of the house only now and then, so
you find it home on most visits even when Brain is busy all day. An outing
lasts 15 minutes; then it comes home by itself, the Work still running, and
stays home for at least 45 minutes before it goes out again. Under nonstop Work
it is home about three quarters of the time. Each Work can take it out twice:
when it starts (once it has run for 15 seconds, so a Work over in moments never
does) and once more after 45 minutes. A Work that starts while the cat rests
waits; after 30 minutes it is no longer news and lets the start go. The
souvenir does not depend on outings: every accepted Work brings one.

The cat does not go out at all until you have met it: it stays in until you
have petted and fed it once, so the first visit always finds it home.

While it is out, a note on its rug shows where it went and when it will be
back. Tap it to see that outing's postcard, or the place itself if the card has
not come yet.

## The first visit

Nothing explains itself in words. The cat greets you, then shows you one thing
at a time by walking over to it and thinking of it while it glows:

1. a ring around the cat: pet it;
2. the letters on the doormat: its welcome note;
3. the bowl on the dining table: feed it (it starts with two fish to share);
4. the window: the yard, where today's treats are waiting;
5. the satchel on the peg: pack a snack for its next trip.

After you first pet it, a card asks its name. The name is yours to give, up to
16 letters, given once; it signs its letters and postcards with it. Close the
card and it asks again later.

Each step is shown once, and the daemon remembers which ones you have done and
which new things you have already met, so another device does not show them
again. A hobby's prop that is new later introduces itself the same way.

## While you were away

Each time you come back, the cat has kept a diary. If you were gone an hour or
more, or something happened meanwhile, a page from the calendar on its wall
opens first, in its hand, with a few lines:

- what it did at home in the parts of the day that went by (tea at dawn, the
  desk in the morning, lunch, an afternoon at a hobby it knows, supper, the
  lamp, sleep);
- where it went and, if it was today, when it was back;
- what it brought home and where in the room it put it;
- a friend who dropped by and the snack it left;
- what it added to the room as you grew closer (a framed postcard, a painting,
  a cushion, bunting), with a small picture of it;
- a hobby it took up;
- the postcards that came, as small photos: tap one to read it.

Close the page and the room shows what it wrote about: each new souvenir,
framed card and new piece of the room shines once, and the letters on the doormat glow. A red ring stays
on the calendar; tap the calendar to read the page again. Opening the cat again
within a few minutes is the same visit and shows no page. The page is written
in the browser from what the daemon already keeps (when you were last here, the
cat's moments, its postcards and souvenirs), so it needs no state of its own.

## What you can tap

- **The cat** answers every tap: a purr, a look, a little hop. Busy at one of
  its places, it turns round and comes over. When you come back after a while
  it does not notice you at once: it finishes what it is doing, then greets
  you, sooner after a long gap, and it may ask for the food you gave it last.
  Asleep, it sleeps on.
- **The bowl** opens the pantry. It eats two meals a day and then shakes its head.
  Food left while it is away waits in the bowl, and it eats it when it is home.
  The food it has been given most becomes its favourite.
- **The yarn** is a game it loves.
- **A hobby's prop** (the toolbox, easel, newspaper, ukulele, watering can,
  letters, telescope), or the stove for cooking, asks it to do that hobby. Once
  a day gardening brings a berry to the pantry and letters bring a letter to
  the doormat.
- **The letters on the doormat** show a new postcard and keep it in the
  **album**; with none waiting they open the album.
- **The souvenirs** stand where it put them around the room: a lantern by the
  bed, a teapot on the stove, a bell on the peg. Tap one to see the Work it came
  from and the hobby that trip taught. **The shelf** lists them all.
- **The window** is the yard. A few treats grow each day: pick them up, and tap
  one in the bag to pack it before the cat leaves. Once it fishes, it sits by
  the pond by day (tap it: once a day it catches a fish); once it camps, its tent
  stands there at night.
- **The picture frames** on the wall show its latest postcards.
- **The bed** asks it for a nap.
- **The calendar** opens its diary page for this visit.
- **Visitors** come now and then while you watch: a bird lands on the round
  window, and from level 4 a friend (a frog, a rabbit, a hedgehog, a snail, an
  owl at night) walks in at the door. Each leaves the snack it likes for the
  pantry.

## The bond

The bond shows only in the home: more frames on the wall, more things in the
room, more hobbies, more souvenirs, friends at the door. There are no numbers on screen. Underneath, care counts stars, a few
a day for each kind so it cannot be ground:

| Care | Stars |
| --- | --- |
| Pet it | 1, three times a day |
| Feed it | 2, twice a day; one more for its favourite |
| Play with the yarn | 2, twice a day |
| Ask for a hobby | 1, three times a day |
| Read a postcard | 1, four times a day |
| Keep a visitor's gift | 1, twice a day |
| An accepted Work | 3 when it comes home, 2 more if you packed it a snack |

There are ten levels (at 0, 6, 16, 30, 48, 70, 96, 126, 160 and 200 stars). A
level lets the cat hold one more hobby and adds something to its home: a
picture frame at levels 2 and 6, its own painting at 3, a cushion for friends
at 5, bunting at 7. It grows one more treat in the yard at levels 3 and 6, and
from level 4 it meets friends on its trips, friends drop by at home, and now
and then it finds a rare place. Days you miss
cost nothing: nothing decays, and a new day simply allows new stars.

### Hobbies

Each place teaches a hobby: the workshop mending, the harbour fishing, the studio
painting, the library the newspaper, the garden gardening, the observatory
stargazing, the market and the kitchen cooking, the post office and the night
desk letters, the station music, and the trail camping. An accepted Work teaches
its place's hobby if the bond has room for one more; otherwise the cat keeps it
in mind and takes it up at the next level. A new hobby brings its prop into the
room (fishing and camping happen in the yard, so the window glows until you
look), and once the cat knows it, it also does it on its own and on its
postcards.

### Memory and mood

The daemon keeps the cat's last 60 moments (petted, fed what, played, which
hobby, learnt, levelled, left, came back, a gift) and a mood: away, excited
(just home), missed (your first visit after most of a day), full, happy (cared
for lately) or calm. The page uses them for its greeting and its requests. They
are where the cat's words will come from once it can talk; there is no chat yet.

## Postcards

A postcard is composed, not painted: an empty picture of a place, the hour as a
wash of light, the day's weather, the cat doing something there, a friend it
met, and the snack it took. The daemon picks each piece and keeps the choice,
so a card looks the same every time it is opened, on every device.

- **The place** comes from the Work's title by fixed keywords (studio, harbour,
  library, workshop, garden, observatory, market, kitchen, post office, station,
  night desk, and a trail for anything else), so it works offline and cannot
  leak: only the place leaves the title, never its words. A cat at level 4 or
  more sometimes writes a long trip's card from a rare place instead: the
  lighthouse, a snowy hot spring, the flower hills, a moonlit lake.
- **What it does** is one of the place's own things. It favours the hobby the
  place teaches, then hobbies it knows; it never shows another hobby it has not
  met. Some cards are candid, as if a friend took the photo: the cat seen from
  behind, looking out at the view, or caught close up walking off with its
  bundle.
- **The hour** is when the card was sent (dawn, day, dusk, night); the
  observatory, the night desk and the lake are only ever at night and the
  lighthouse at dusk. **The weather** is one per day; snow falls only from
  December to February, and indoors it is only in the words.
- **Friends** (a frog, a snail, a rabbit, a hedgehog, a bird by day, an owl at
  night) join from level 4, more often as the bond grows. Each has a snack it
  likes (the frog and the owl fish, the bird and the hedgehog berries, the
  rabbit and the snail catnip): pack that snack and that friend is likely to
  come along. While the cat is home, after you have been away three hours or
  more, a friend may drop by and leave its snack in the pantry; the diary says
  who came.

No two cards in the album show the same scene (place, doing, hour, weather and
friend): a crowded album varies the weather and brings friends to keep them
apart. The line is written in English from the same pieces. Besides trip
cards, the cat writes a welcome note on the first visit and, once it has taken
up letters, a letter from home.

A postcard comes from each outing, so at most one when a Work starts and one
when it runs long, and at least 10 minutes pass between any two, so many Works
starting at once never flood the album. A Work that is already running when the daemon
first learns of the cat sends nothing: history is not backfilled.

The souvenir keeps the title of your own Work, on your own shelf only.

## The living room

- **At home** it keeps a day like a person's, by the phone's clock: tea at dawn
  (from about 5:30), writing at its desk in the morning, lunch at noon, a nap or
  a hobby in the afternoon, supper at the stove from 5 pm, reading by the lamp
  in the evening, and bed from 10:30 pm. Each is painted into the room, often
  with its back to you. Between them it sits on the rug to read, play with
  yarn, or do a hobby it knows. What it picks follows the hour, chance and what
  you ask of it most.
- **Light**: dusk from about 6 pm, night by 8:30 pm, dawn from about 5:30 am;
  sunlight falls through the window by day.
- **Small life**: dust in the sunbeam, a butterfly, crickets at night, and the
  visiting bird.
- **Sound** is made in the page, with no audio files: a quiet room tone, paper,
  a purr, a chime, small sounds for each hobby. It starts with the
  first tap and stops whenever the tab is not on screen. There is no switch.

## Where it lives

The daemon keeps the state per computer (`catpark/state.json` in the state
directory) and derives it from Brain's snapshots and the Work record. Every
postcard and souvenir is keyed by Work and written to disk before it is
announced, so a restart repeats nothing. App and web UI show the same state
because both read it from the daemon over the connection they already have:
`cat_get` and `cat_state`, and `cat_act` with one of a fixed list of acts
(`visit`, `pet`, `feed`, `play`, `hobby`, `collect`, `pack`, `seen`, `gift`,
`learn`, `name`); the daemon checks every act and its argument and ignores the rest.
A state written by an older version of the game is kept beside it
(`state.json.v1`) and a new cat starts.

The game is one web page the daemon carries (`/cat/`). The phone cannot open a
daemon URL in a WebView (its connection is a pinned tunnel on a port that
changes per launch), so the app downloads the page with signed requests into a
cache keyed by its version, checks each file against the manifest's digest, and
opens it from disk. What is missing arrives as one tar (`/cat/bundle.tar`, built
from the same manifest) because every round trip over a tunnel costs a second
or more and the bundle is over a hundred files; a computer without the archive
is read file by file, and every entry is checked against the manifest either
way. The first open shows a progress line and always ends, in the house or in a
retry, within two minutes. The page holds no credentials and has no network access; it
can ask the app only for the acts above. After the first visit it opens offline
with the last state it knew. The web UI frames the same page from `/cat/`: the
web UI's content security policy allows `frame-src 'self'`, and only `/cat/`
allows being framed, by the web UI itself.

The cat's world is one file, `daemon/catpark/web/catalog.js`: the levels, foods,
hobbies, places with where the cat stands in each picture, rare places, friends,
weathers, and every word the cat writes. The page draws from it and the daemon
reads the same file to compose postcards. Like the rest of the product it is
English only; tests fail if any place, act, friend, food or hobby lacks a picture
or a word, or if another language creeps into the page. The pictures are
generated art in the page's `a/` folder.

The handwritten line uses a subset of LXGW WenKai (SIL OFL 1.1); its notice is in
the repository's third-party assets list.

To look at the room at another hour, open the page with `?hour=` and an hour
from 0 to 23 (for example `/cat/?hour=19`); it changes only what the page
shows.
